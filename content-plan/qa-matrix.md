# BMX Fitment and Identification Matrix

One hundred questions a person actually types in right before they buy, sell, or try to identify a used BMX part. Each one is meant to become a short standalone page that answers the question directly and then points at whatever we have in stock that fits. Confidence is marked `solid` where the answer is a stable fact worth writing straight away, and `verify` where Joe should confirm dimensions, year ranges, or brand specifics against a primary source before the page goes live.

### The 15 to write first

These are picked for a mix of search demand and how directly they lead to someone buying a part. Every one of them is a question asked by a person who is about to hand over money.

1. Q001 What bottom bracket does my BMX frame take?
2. Q081 How do I tell if a BMX frame is chromoly or hi-ten?
3. Q028 How do I tell if a BMX rear hub is a cassette, freecoaster, or freewheel?
4. Q012 What size headset does a BMX bike use?
5. Q024 What is the difference between a 14mm and a 3/8 inch BMX rear axle?
6. Q059 What diameter seat post does my BMX frame take?
7. Q074 What thread size are BMX pedals, 9/16 or 1/2 inch?
8. Q008 How do I tell a one piece crank from a three piece crank?
9. Q077 What axle size do BMX pegs need?
10. Q002 What is the difference between American, Mid, and Spanish bottom brackets?
11. Q090 What years count as old school, mid school, and new school BMX?
12. Q036 How do I tell what size driver is on my BMX hub?
13. Q061 What is the difference between a pivotal, railed, and tripod BMX seat?
14. Q096 What should I check before buying a used BMX frame?
15. Q006 What is the difference between 19mm, 22mm, and 24mm BMX crank spindles?

Q001, Q012, Q059 and Q074 are the four that convert hardest. Someone asking them has a frame in front of them and a part missing from it. Each of those pages should end with the matching category of stock rather than a generic link to the eBay store.

### Overlap with dictionary-style terms

A number of these questions sit right next to a term that belongs in a glossary rather than a Q and A page. The rule that keeps the two sets from competing: the dictionary entry defines the word in two or three sentences and links out, the Q and A page answers a decision and links back to the definition. Do not let a dictionary entry grow into a fitment guide.

Terms that will need an entry and a cross-link:

- Mid, American, Spanish, Euro (bottom bracket standards) against Q001 to Q007
- Integrated headset, press in headset, campy spec against Q012 and Q013
- Cassette hub, freecoaster, freewheel, driver against Q028, Q035 and Q036
- Male axle, female axle, bolt on axle against Q026 and Q078
- Pivotal, railed, tripod, seat mast against Q056, Q061 and Q062
- Gyro, detangler, 990 mount, U-brake against Q042 to Q046
- Chromoly, hi-ten, 4130, heat treated, butted tubing against Q081 to Q083
- Old school, mid school, new school against Q058, Q090 and Q092
- Front load, top load, stem stack against Q066
- Half link, gear inches, micro drive against Q033, Q034 and Q038

Where a term and a question have effectively the same title, keep the question. It matches how people search and how an assistant is asked, and the dictionary entry can carry the shorter noun form.

### Honesty note on the count

All 100 are questions I would expect to see typed into a search box or asked of an assistant. I stopped adding rather than padding in two places: brakes and detanglers, and era and dating. Both topics have a limited number of genuinely distinct questions, and splitting them further would only have produced pages competing with each other. If more volume is wanted later, the honest way to get it is brand and model specific pages (a named frame, a named hub) rather than more generic fitment questions.

Fifty five of the hundred are marked `verify`. That is deliberate. Anything involving a millimetre figure, a thread pitch, a year range, or a brand's own naming needs Joe to check it against a catalogue, a spec sheet, or a part in hand before it is published. A wrong number on a fitment page costs a return and a bad feedback score, which is worse than not having the page.
