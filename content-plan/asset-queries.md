## Notes

### Terms where video will not exist or will not help

Do not spend time hunting video for these. Some are abstract, some are single facts that a diagram or a sentence covers better than any clip, and some only exist on YouTube as generic bike content with nothing BMX about it. Use the image column for these rows and leave the video column blank.

- `yield-strength`. A materials science concept. Any video found will be an engineering lecture with no bike in it. The stress strain diagram does the whole job.
- `zinc-plated-hardware`. Nobody has made a video about plating on BMX bolts. A close-up photo pair of zinc against chrome is the entire asset.
- `quad-butted-tubing`. Marketing term. General butted tubing video exists and is fine, but it will not say the word quad. Use the diagram.
- `keyed-washer`. Too small a component for anyone to have filmed on its own.
- `jam-nut`. Generic mechanical fastening term. Covered incidentally inside hub servicing videos but never as its own subject.
- `knurled-bar-clamp`. A surface texture. A photo covers it.
- `non-drive-side`. A direction, not a part. Do not waste a search.
- `frame-reach`. Reach and stack video is all road and mountain bike. BMX frames are rarely specced this way, so a video would confuse rather than help.
- `standover-height` and `wheelbase`. Both are one-line measurements. The geometry diagram is the asset.
- `junior-bmx-frame-size` and `pro-xl-frame-size`. Race size classes are a chart, not a film. There is BMX race sizing video, but it is all rider-fit advice rather than the class definitions these pages need.
- `zero-offset-seat-post`. Setback video exists for road bikes only, and the BMX case is trivial.
- `axle-nuts-and-bolts`. Only exists as a two second moment inside wheel install videos.
- `x-up`. Video is abundant but it is all trick tutorial. It will not support the fitment point the page is making, which is why riders want a detangler. Use a still frame instead.
- `old-school-bmx` and `mid-school-bmx` are the exception to the era rule. Both have genuinely good collector and era-guide video, so search those two even though the sizing and dating terms around them do not.

Two more worth flagging as low value rather than impossible: `spoke-nipple` and `adjustable-cone` both only appear inside longer wheelbuilding and hub servicing videos. If you already have a good wheelbuild video for `spoke-lacing-pattern`, reuse it here rather than searching again.

### Shortlist of 15 where video adds the most

These are the rows where a real video does something no amount of text can. All fifteen are procedures, sounds, or side-by-side comparisons, and all fifteen almost certainly have good BMX-specific content already on YouTube.

1. `mid-bottom-bracket`. Pressing bearings in and out is the single most demonstrable BMX job there is.
2. `Q001` What bottom bracket does my BMX frame take. Someone holding calipers to a shell answers this in thirty seconds.
3. `Q003` How do I measure a BMX bottom bracket shell. Same reason, and it is the measurement page everything else links into.
4. `freecoaster`. The internal clutch and the slack are impossible to explain in text and obvious on film.
5. `Q028` Cassette, freecoaster, or freewheel. The spin-backwards test is a video, not a paragraph.
6. `cassette-driver`. Driver removal is fiddly and brand-specific, and seeing it done prevents damage.
7. `gyro-detangler` and `Q047` gyro cable length. Gyro setup is the classic job people get wrong, and there is plenty of good shop footage.
8. `Q008` One piece versus three piece cranks. A direct visual comparison, instantly settled on camera.
9. `one-piece-crank`. Ashtabula removal needs the technique shown or people wreck the threads.
10. `spoke-lacing-pattern`. Wheelbuilding is one of the best-covered subjects on YouTube and a full lacing video carries three of these pages.
11. `Q064` Stuck seat post removal. A rescue job that people search in a panic, and the methods only make sense demonstrated.
12. `Q073` Removing grips without wrecking them. The compressed air trick is pure video.
13. `Q081` Chromoly or hi-ten. Weight comparisons and sticker walkthroughs on camera settle this better than any spec claim.
14. `Q055` and `Q096` Checking a used frame for cracks and what to check before buying. Someone pointing at the actual failure points on a real frame is the most useful asset on the whole site.
15. `u-brake` and `Q043`. U-brake setup and straddle cable adjustment is a job most people only half understand, and it is well filmed.

If you only have an afternoon, do numbers 1 to 6 first. They cover the four dictionary pages and the two questions flagged as the hardest converting in the source files.
