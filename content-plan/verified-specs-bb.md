# Verified Specs: BMX Bottom Brackets, Crank Spindles, Axles and Dropout Spacing

Verification date: 2026-08-28

Status key:
- `CONFIRMED` = two or more independent good sources agree
- `SINGLE SOURCE` = only one usable source found
- `CONFLICTING` = sources disagree, both figures given
- `UNVERIFIED` = no decent source found

Source quality note: BMX component manufacturers used here are Odyssey, Profile Racing, GSport, Box Components and Rant (via a retailer product spec). Park Tool and Sheldon Brown are used as general bicycle technical references. Framebuilder Supply and Bicycle Fabrication Supply are frame tubing suppliers, which makes them useful for raw shell dimensions. Dan's Comp, Source BMX, Albe's, Empire BMX and SkatePro are retailers with real spec content. TBB-BIKE and BMX Direct are BMX shops with technical pages. BMX Union is BMX media.

## 1. Bottom bracket standards

### 1.1 American

**Claim: frame shell inner diameter**

| Figure | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 51.5 mm | [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bottom-bracket-standards-and-terminology) | stated as "51.5 mm cup" |
| 51.5 mm | [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) | "diameter: 51,5mm" |
| 51.3 mm (2.02 in) | [Sheldon Brown](https://www.sheldonbrown.com/opc.html) | "One-piece cranks fit only the unthreaded shells 51.3 mm (2.02") in diameter" |
| 51 mm | [SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/c599.htm) | rounded, given as bearing outer diameter |

Status: `CONFLICTING`. The cluster is 51.3 mm to 51.5 mm. Park Tool and TBB-BIKE agree on 51.5 mm, Sheldon Brown says 51.3 mm. Treat as "roughly 51.3 to 51.5 mm" rather than a single exact number. Note that some sources quote the cup outer diameter and some quote the shell bore, which may be the origin of the spread.

**Claim: typical frame shell width**

- Figure: 68 mm
- Source: [Bicycle Fabrication Supply, "AMERICAN BOTTOM BRACKET SHELL 68MM X 57MM Ø"](https://www.bikefabsupply.com/bottom-brackets/p/68mm-american-bb-shell) (product title; the page body could not be fetched, robots.txt disallowed)
- Status: `SINGLE SOURCE`. The 57 mm figure in that title is the shell outer diameter, not the bore.

**Claim: bearing type and how it mounts**

- Answer: two variants. Sealed American uses cartridge bearings held in cups that are pressed into the frame. Unsealed American uses threaded cups with loose ball retainers, for one-piece Ashtabula cranks.
- Sources: [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/bmx-bottom-bracket-types/cp481) states American is "larger diameter and can use sealed or unsealed bearings. Sealed American BB's use the same bearings as a Mid BB, but they include a cup that must be pressed in to the frame". [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bottom-bracket-standards-and-terminology) describes it as pressed into the frame. [Sheldon Brown](https://www.sheldonbrown.com/opc.html) covers the one-piece retainer version.
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: American cup thread pitch for one-piece cranks**

- Answer: 24 TPI is the common size, 28 TPI exists for some older Schwinn and Mongoose bikes.
- Sources: [Albe's BMX](https://www.albes.com/collections/bmx-bottom-brackets) notes "24TPI for most stuff" and "28TPI works for old Mongoose bikes"; [BMXGuru](https://www.bmxguru.com/products/28-tpi-bottom-bracket-f-one-piece-crank-28tpi-for-schwinn-mongoose) lists a 28 TPI version for Schwinn and Mongoose alongside a [24 TPI version](https://www.bmxguru.com/products/american-bottom-bracket-24tpi-f-one-piece-crank-opc).
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: cartridge bearing used in a sealed American BB**

- Answer: the same bearing as a Mid BB. Outer diameter 41.3 mm, width 11 mm, marked R12 2RS in the 19 mm bore version.
- Sources: [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) gives "bearings diameter: 41,3mm", "bearings width: 11mm", "R12 2RS // ISK" for the USA size; [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/bmx-bottom-bracket-types/cp481) confirms American sealed uses the same bearings as Mid; [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/mid-american-bottom-bracket-bearing/) sells one bearing as a combined "Mid/American" part, OD 1.625 in, width .438 in.
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: spindle diameters accommodated**

- Answer: Odyssey's own American BB is offered in 19 mm only. Profile's Mid/American bearing is offered in 19 mm and 22 mm. SkatePro lists 19, 22 and 24 mm as available across the standards generally.
- Sources: [Odyssey shop](https://shop.odysseybmx.com/collections/odyssey-bottom-brackets) lists "American BB Silver" with 19 mm spindle compatibility only; [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/mid-american-bottom-bracket-bearing/) "19mm or 22mm Spindles"; [SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/c599.htm) "19 mm, 22 mm and 24 mm".
- Status: `CONFIRMED` for 19 mm and 22 mm. 24 mm in an American shell is `UNVERIFIED` as a currently offered product.

**Claim: era**

- Answer: the original BMX standard, used with one-piece Ashtabula cranks and later sealed conversions. Still present on mid-school frames of the late 1990s and early 2000s. Largely superseded.
- Sources: [BMX Union](https://bmxunion.com/evolutions-bmx-frames/) refers to American bottom brackets on mid-school era frames and notes the American design required "cups that had to be pressed in first"; [GSport](https://gsportbmx.com/2004/06/bottom-bracket-compendium/) says the USA bottom bracket has been around "as long as the sport itself"; [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) says "It is no longer used"; [Sheldon Brown](https://www.sheldonbrown.com/opc.html) says one-piece cranks are "mainly found on older American-made bicycles, and children's bicycles made for the U.S. market".
- Status: `CONFIRMED` as a general narrative. No source gave firm start and end years, so do not publish specific dates.

### 1.2 Mid

**Claim: frame shell inner diameter**

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| 41.2 mm (1 5/8 in) | [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bottom-bracket-standards-and-terminology) |
| 41.25 mm | [Framebuilder Supply, steel Mid shell PF41](https://framebuildersupply.com/products/steel-mid-bottom-bracket-shell-press-fit-68mm) ("41.25 ID, 44.4mm OD") |
| 41.3 mm | [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) |
| 41.275 mm (1 5/8 in) | [soulcycle BMX](https://soulcycle.com/pages/bottom-bracket-sizes) |
| 41 mm | [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/), [SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/c599.htm) |

- Status: `CONFIRMED`. All sources are the same number to rounding. The exact value is 1 5/8 inch, which is 41.275 mm. Safe to publish as "41.3 mm (1 5/8 inch)".

**Claim: typical frame shell width**

- Figure: 68 mm typical, 73 mm also supported
- Sources: [Framebuilder Supply](https://framebuildersupply.com/products/steel-mid-bottom-bracket-shell-press-fit-68mm) sells the Mid shell in 68 mm; [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/22mm-midspanish-bb-kit/) states "Spanish and Mid bottom brackets are for either 68mm or 73mm bottom bracket shells."
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: bearing type and how it mounts**

- Answer: sealed cartridge bearings press directly into the frame shell. No cups.
- Sources: [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/bmx-bottom-bracket-types/cp481) "uses sealed bearings that press directly in to the frame, leaving a flush fit"; [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bottom-bracket-standards-and-terminology) describes a 41.2 mm cartridge bearing press fit; [BMX Union](https://bmxunion.com/evolutions-bmx-frames/) "The Mid bottom bracket shell utilized the bearings from an American bottom bracket, but eliminated the need for cups."
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: cartridge bearing part numbers**

| Spindle bore | Part number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 19 mm | R12 2RS, ID 19 mm, OD 41 mm | [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/) |
| 22 mm | R12RS-22, ID 22 mm, OD 41 mm | [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/) |
| 22 mm | "R12 RSD 22", diameter 41.3 mm, width 11 mm | [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) |
| 24 mm | listed as "TBA", ID 24 mm, OD 41 mm | [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/) |

- Bearing physical size from a manufacturer: [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/mid-american-bottom-bracket-bearing/) gives ID .75 in (19 mm) or 22 mm, OD 1.625 in, width .438 in. That width is 11.13 mm, consistent with TBB-BIKE's 11 mm.
- Status: `CONFIRMED` for the R12 family designation for 19 mm and 22 mm (two independent sources for the 22 mm, one for the 19 mm plus the matching Profile dimensions). `SINGLE SOURCE` and incomplete for the 24 mm part number, since BMX Direct lists it as TBA. Do not publish a 24 mm Mid bearing part number.

**Claim: spindle diameters accommodated**

- Answer: 19 mm and 22 mm are the mainstream offerings. 24 mm bore Mid bearings are sold.
- Sources: [Odyssey shop](https://shop.odysseybmx.com/collections/odyssey-bottom-brackets) lists "BMX Mid BB" as "Press-fit Mid" with 19 mm or 22 mm; [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/mid-american-bottom-bracket-bearing/) 19 mm or 22 mm; [Empire BMX](https://www.empirebmx.com/products/empire-bmx-mid-bottom-bracket-bearing) sells a Mid BB replacement bearing in "19mm, 22mm, or 24mm I.D."
- Status: `CONFIRMED` for 19 mm and 22 mm. `SINGLE SOURCE` for 24 mm in Mid (Empire BMX only).

**Claim: era**

- Answer: introduced after the Euro standard, credited to FBM. Now the dominant BMX standard.
- Sources: [BMX Union](https://bmxunion.com/evolutions-bmx-frames/) credits FBM with introducing it on the "PW Moto frame" and later the Capone; [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) calls it the "Most used bottom bracket in BMX".
- Status: `CONFIRMED` as a narrative. No source gave a specific introduction year. Do not publish a year.

### 1.3 Spanish

**Claim: frame shell inner diameter**

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| nominally 37 mm | [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bottom-bracket-standards-and-terminology) |
| 37 mm | [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) |
| 37 mm bearing | [GSport](https://gsportbmx.com/2004/06/bottom-bracket-compendium/) |
| 37 mm | [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/), [soulcycle](https://soulcycle.com/pages/bottom-bracket-sizes), [SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/c599.htm) |

- Status: `CONFIRMED` at 37 mm.

**Claim: typical frame shell width**

- Figure: 68 mm or 73 mm
- Source: [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/19mm-midspanish-bottom-bracket/) "Spanish and Mid bottom brackets are for either 68mm or 73mm bottom bracket shells."
- Status: `SINGLE SOURCE`. This is a manufacturer statement, so it is trustworthy, but no second source confirmed it.

**Claim: bearing type and how it mounts**

- Answer: sealed cartridge bearing presses directly into the frame, no cups. Same concept as Mid, smaller diameter.
- Sources: [GSport](https://gsportbmx.com/2004/06/bottom-bracket-compendium/) describes a "cartridge bearing pressing straight into the frame"; [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/bmx-bottom-bracket-types/cp481) "work the same as Mid, they just use a slightly smaller diameter"; [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bottom-bracket-standards-and-terminology) bearing "pressed directly into the frame".
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: cartridge bearing part numbers**

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| "6904" for both 19 mm and 22 mm bore, OD 37 mm | [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/) |
| "22379 - 2RS // NBK", diameter 37 mm, width 9 mm | [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) |

- Status: `CONFLICTING`, and see the cautions section. A standard 6904 bearing is 20 x 37 x 9 mm, so a 19 mm or 22 mm bore bearing at 37 mm OD is not a stock 6904. TBB-BIKE's "22379" reads as 22 x 37 x 9, which is dimensionally coherent for a 22 mm spindle. BMX Direct itself notes these are special sizes not available from standard bearing shops. Recommend publishing the dimensions (37 mm OD, 9 mm width) rather than a part number.
- Bearing width 9 mm: `SINGLE SOURCE` (TBB-BIKE).

**Claim: spindle diameters accommodated**

- Answer: 19 mm and 22 mm are what manufacturers sell. GSport's 2004 article speculated about spindles up to 25 mm.
- Sources: [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/22mm-midspanish-bb-kit/) and [Profile Racing 19 mm](https://www.profileracing.com/product/19mm-midspanish-bottom-bracket/); [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/) lists only 19 mm and 22 mm for Spanish; [soulcycle](https://soulcycle.com/pages/bottom-bracket-sizes) lists 19, 22 and 24 mm for Spanish.
- Status: `CONFIRMED` for 19 mm and 22 mm. 24 mm in a Spanish shell is `SINGLE SOURCE` (soulcycle) and physically marginal, since bearing wall thickness at 37 mm OD is already thin. Do not present 24 mm Spanish as a normal option.

**Claim: era**

- Answer: a later, lighter alternative to Mid. GSport described it as still in development at the time of their 2004 article.
- Sources: [GSport](https://gsportbmx.com/2004/06/bottom-bracket-compendium/) (article dated June 2004, describes Spanish as in a development stage); [BMX Union](https://bmxunion.com/evolutions-bmx-frames/) says Spanish appeared "around this time period" alongside Mid; [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) says it is used by lightweight frame manufacturers.
- Status: `CONFIRMED` that it is a mid 2000s onward standard, still in use but less common than Mid. The GSport 2004 article date is the only hard date anchor found.

### 1.4 Euro (threaded)

**Claim: thread specification**

- Figure: 1.37 inch x 24 TPI, the same as English or BSA
- Sources: [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bmx-three-piece-crank-service) "euro threaded bottom bracket standard (1.37″ x 24 TPI)"; [Box Components](https://boxcomponents.com/products/box-two-bottom-bracket-24mm) "1.37 Inch x 24 TPI (English)"; Rant Bang Ur Euro BB spec "Euro style BMX bottom bracket 1.37` x 24tpi" via [Modern Bike](https://www.modernbike.com/rant-bang-ur-euro-bmx-bottom-bracket-black)
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: frame shell inner diameter**

- Figure: approximately 34.8 mm, derived from the 1.37 inch thread major diameter
- Source: [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bottom-bracket-standards-and-terminology) associates the BMX "Euro" name with the English threading standard, 1.37 in x 24 TPI, roughly 34.8 mm shell diameter
- Status: `SINGLE SOURCE`. Note this is the thread diameter, not a plain bore, so it is not directly comparable to the press-fit numbers above.

**Claim: typical frame shell width**

- Figure: 68 mm on BMX. 73 mm on MTB and dirt jump frames.
- Sources: [Park Tool](https://www.parktool.com/en-us/blog/repair-help/bmx-three-piece-crank-service) "68mm and 73mm"; [Odyssey shop](https://shop.odysseybmx.com/collections/odyssey-bottom-brackets) lists "BMX Euro BB" for "68mm shells" and a separate "MTB/DJ Euro BB" for "73mm shells"; Rant Euro BB "Shell: 68mm" via [Modern Bike](https://www.modernbike.com/rant-bang-ur-euro-bmx-bottom-bracket-black)
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: bearing type and how it mounts**

- Answer: threaded cups screw into the frame and house the bearings.
- Sources: [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/bmx-bottom-bracket-types/cp481) "use cups that thread in to the frame. These cups house the bearings"; [GSport](https://gsportbmx.com/2004/06/bottom-bracket-compendium/) describes threaded cups screwed into the frame.
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: bearing outer diameter and part numbers**

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| bearing diameter 31 mm, width 6.9 mm, marked "22x31x72xRS // ISK or 1905137 - 2RS // NBK" | [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) |
| OD 32 mm, 22 mm bore part "8539"; 19 mm listed as TBA | [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/) |
| "35 mm threaded" | [SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/c599.htm) |

- Status: `CONFLICTING`. Three sources, three different numbers (31, 32, 35 mm). The 35 mm figure is likely the shell thread size rather than the bearing. Do not publish a Euro bearing outer diameter as fact. Euro BB bearings sit inside a cup, so the bearing size depends on the cup design, which may be why sources disagree.

**Claim: spindle diameters accommodated**

- Answer: 19 mm and 22 mm on Odyssey's BMX Euro BB. 22 mm only on their MTB/DJ 73 mm version. 24 mm exists on external-bearing BSA/Euro race bottom brackets.
- Sources: [Odyssey shop](https://shop.odysseybmx.com/collections/odyssey-bottom-brackets); Rant Euro BB "19mm spindle" via [Modern Bike](https://www.modernbike.com/rant-bang-ur-euro-bmx-bottom-bracket-black); [Box Components Box Two 24mm](https://boxcomponents.com/products/box-two-bottom-bracket-24mm) is a BSA/Euro threaded 68/73 mm unit with a 24 mm spindle
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: era**

- Answer: an early three-piece-crank alternative to American, still available but uncommon on modern freestyle frames. Widely used on BMX race frames with external bearing 24 mm systems.
- Sources: [BMX Union](https://bmxunion.com/evolutions-bmx-frames/) identifies the Kink Cielencki as "one of the first frames with the Euro bottom bracket" and places the shift right before Mid appeared; [TBB-BIKE](https://www.tbb-bike.com/how-to-bottom-brackets/) says Euro is "rarely used today"; [GSport](https://gsportbmx.com/2004/06/bottom-bracket-compendium/) says Euro has been around "even longer than the USA bottom bracket"
- Status: `CONFLICTING` on origin. GSport says Euro predates the USA bottom bracket in general bicycle terms, BMX Union describes it arriving in BMX around the late 1990s or early 2000s. These are reconcilable if you separate the general bicycle threading standard from its adoption in BMX, but the sources do not say so explicitly. No firm year found for the Kink Cielencki. Do not publish a year.

## 2. Crank spindles

**Claim: the three common BMX spindle diameters are 19 mm, 22 mm and 24 mm**

- Answer: correct for BMX freestyle. NOT complete for BMX race.
- Sources: [SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/c599.htm) "19 mm, 22 mm and 24 mm"; [soulcycle](https://soulcycle.com/pages/bottom-bracket-sizes) lists 19, 22 and 24 mm against every BB standard; [Dan's Comp crank guide](https://www.danscomp.com/what-to-know-when-buying-new-bmx-cranks/cp1711) discusses 19, 22 and 24 mm; [Empire BMX](https://www.empirebmx.com/products/empire-bmx-mid-bottom-bracket-bearing) sells Mid bearings in 19, 22 and 24 mm ID.
- Important qualifier: [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/bmx-bottom-bracket-types/cp481) states that "freestyle uses 19mm and 22mm spindle sizes, while racing uses 19mm, 22mm, 24mm, 30mm, and 35mm options."
- Status: `CONFIRMED` that 19, 22 and 24 mm are the three common sizes. `CONFIRMED` that the set is NOT complete for BMX race, where 30 mm and 35 mm also exist (`SINGLE SOURCE`, Dan's Comp, for the 30 and 35 mm figures specifically).

**Claim: 24 mm spindle equals 15/16 inch**

- Figure: sprockets are "likely bored for a 24mm (15/16") spindle"
- Source: [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/what-to-know-when-buying-new-bmx-cranks/cp1711)
- Status: `SINGLE SOURCE`. Note 15/16 inch is 23.81 mm, so "24 mm" is a rounded nominal name for an imperial size.

**Claim: which spindle goes with which era and riding style**

| Spindle | What sources say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 19 mm | "Most complete bikes come with a 19mm diameter spindle. This is also very common amongst aftermarket cranks." Described as the standard and "plenty strong." | [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/what-to-know-when-buying-new-bmx-cranks/cp1711) |
| 19 mm | "19 mm axles are the most common" | [SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/c599.htm) |
| 22 mm | "a common upgrade for those riders looking for a bit of extra durability", it "seemed to be the perfect medium" after industry experimentation | [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/what-to-know-when-buying-new-bmx-cranks/cp1711) |
| 24 mm | created "a stronger spindle" but "a weaker bearing" because the bearing inner diameter became too large | [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/what-to-know-when-buying-new-bmx-cranks/cp1711) |
| 24 mm | mainly seen on BSA/Euro external-bearing race systems | [Box Components](https://boxcomponents.com/products/box-two-bottom-bracket-24mm), [Dan's Comp race BB category](https://www.danscomp.com/bottom-brackets-race-parts/c16143) |

- Status: `CONFIRMED` for 19 mm as the default and 22 mm as the durability upgrade. `SINGLE SOURCE` for the specific claim that 24 mm trades spindle strength for bearing weakness (Dan's Comp only).
- Era mapping to specific years: `UNVERIFIED`. No source gave dates for when 19 mm or 22 mm became common. Do not publish years.

**Claim: how spindle diameter relates to bottom bracket bearing bore**

- Answer: the spindle diameter is the bearing inner diameter. The bottom bracket standard determines the bearing outer diameter. These are two independent numbers.
- Source: [BMX Direct](https://bmxdirect.net/bmx-hub-and-crank-bearings-sizes/) states directly: "The main difference between the 2 types is the outer diameter of the bearing. The 'Mid' bearing is 41mm on the outside and a 'Spanish' BB is 37mm." Their table lists bore against outer diameter for each combination. Corroborated by [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/mid-american-bottom-bracket-bearing/), whose Mid/American bearing holds the outer diameter at 1.625 in while the bore changes between 19 mm and 22 mm.
- Status: `CONFIRMED`

**Claim: whether a given spindle can run in a given BB shell, and what changes**

- Answer: yes, within limits. You change the bearings, not the frame. The bearing outer diameter must match the shell standard and the bearing bore must match the spindle. Spacers set the chainline and take up shell width differences.
- Sources: [Profile Racing](https://www.profileracing.com/product/22mm-midspanish-bb-kit/) sells one kit that covers both Mid and Spanish shells at either 68 mm or 73 mm width, and includes "Tube spacer (1), Color anodized cones (2), 5mm Tube spacer (1), 3/16″ Thick spacer (1)"; the [19 mm version](https://www.profileracing.com/product/19mm-midspanish-bottom-bracket/) includes a different spacer set; [Odyssey](https://shop.odysseybmx.com/collections/odyssey-bottom-brackets) lists "Spacers included" on both the Mid and American BBs; [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/what-to-know-when-buying-new-bmx-cranks/cp1711) advises to "make sure the new cranks work with your current bottom bracket, or just go ahead and get a new BB as well."
- Sprocket adaptation: [Dan's Comp](https://www.danscomp.com/what-to-know-when-buying-new-bmx-cranks/cp1711) states sprockets are often bored for 24 mm (15/16 in) "which allows you to use a small adapter for a 22mm or 19mm spindle, but some are simply 19mm."
- Status: `CONFIRMED`
- Caution: no source found stated a hard upper limit on spindle diameter per shell standard. Do not publish a compatibility matrix asserting, for example, that 24 mm cannot fit Spanish. Sell what the manufacturers list.

## 3. Confusion question 1: is a 3/8 inch axle the same as a 10 mm axle?

**Claim: they are different sizes.**

- Answer: different. 3/8 inch is 0.375 inch, which is 9.525 mm. A 10 mm axle is 10.0 mm, about 0.475 mm larger.
- Sources:
  - [BilletBMX](https://billetbmx.com/blogs/news/bmx-axle-nut-thread-sizes-the-3-8-vs-14mm-breakdown-every-rider-needs): "3/8" axles have a shaft diameter of 9.525mm." The same page lists 10 mm (M10) as a separate standard used in "Lightweight race-only setups."
  - [Wheels Manufacturing](https://wheelsmfg.com/pages/hub-tech-help): lists axles available in "9mm, 9.5mm (3/8") and 10mm diameters", treating 3/8 inch and 10 mm as two different sizes.
- Status: `CONFIRMED`
- Exact conversion: 3/8 in = 0.375 in = 9.525 mm. Arithmetic, not a sourced claim, but it agrees with BilletBMX's stated figure.

**Related claim: 3/8 inch axle thread pitch**

- Figures: "26 TPI (the most common)" and "24 TPI (found mostly on coaster brake hubs)"
- Source: [BilletBMX](https://billetbmx.com/blogs/news/bmx-axle-nut-thread-sizes-the-3-8-vs-14mm-breakdown-every-rider-needs)
- Status: `SINGLE SOURCE`

**Related claim: 3/8 inch and 14 mm are not interchangeable**

- Source: [BilletBMX](https://billetbmx.com/blogs/news/bmx-axle-nut-thread-sizes-the-3-8-vs-14mm-breakdown-every-rider-needs): "3/8" and 14mm are NOT interchangeable."
- Status: `SINGLE SOURCE`

**Practical note for listings:** because 3/8 inch and 10 mm are only about half a millimetre apart, sellers routinely use the terms interchangeably and no source found endorses that. If a hub or axle is not marked, the honest listing says which one it measures, or says it was not measured.

## 4. Confusion question 2: BMX rear dropout spacing

This is the distance between the inner faces of the rear dropouts, also called frame spacing or over-locknut dimension. It is a completely separate measurement from axle diameter.

**Claim: modern BMX rear dropout spacing**

- Figure: 110 mm
- Sources:
  - [Odyssey BMX](https://odysseybmx.com/2002/12/61-dropouts/), a manufacturer tech article: dropout width "should be about 110mm in most cases". The same article notes the example frame ended up "at the standard 108mm spacing."

## 6. Wheels: tyres and tubes, batch 1A terms 4 through 20

**Claim: wire bead vs folding bead construction (steel wire vs aramid/Kevlar)**

- Answer: a wire bead tyre uses a steel wire loop at the bead, is heavier and does not fold. A folding bead tyre uses aramid (Kevlar) fibre instead, is lighter, folds flat, and costs more. Both fit the same rim in the same way for the same casing.
- Sources:
  - Kenda's own tyre glossary: [Kenda Tires, Glossary](https://bicycle.kendatire.com/en-us/tire-tech/glossary/), defining wire bead as "a thin steel wire" and folding bead as "an Aramid string," both "the attachment point between the tire and the rim"
  - [WTB, "Wire Bead vs. Folding Bead Tires"](https://www.wtb.com/blogs/wtb/wire-bead-vs-folding-bead-tires-what-s-the-difference-and-which-should-you-choose), corroborating material, weight and cost differences
  - [BikeRadar, "What's the difference between a folding and wire bead tyre?"](https://www.bikeradar.com/features/qa/what-s-the-difference-between-a-folding-and-wire-bead-tyre), independent corroboration of the same material, weight and cost claims
- Status: `CONFIRMED`
- Note: this is a general bicycle tyre construction fact, not BMX-specific in its sourcing (Kenda and WTB are not BMX-only brands), but Kenda does make BMX tyres (K50) and the construction principle applies identically to BMX tyres. No BMX-specific manufacturer contradicts it.

**Claim: bead hook definition (the lip inside the rim wall that retains the tyre bead)**

- Answer: the bead hook is the inward-facing lip on the inner rim wall that grabs the tyre bead and prevents it rolling off the rim under pressure. Standard clincher (hooked) rims have this on both walls. Hookless rims, which have straight walls instead, exist in other categories of cycling.
- Source: [Hunt Bike Wheels, "Hookless vs Hooked: An Explainer"](https://www.huntbikewheels.com/blogs/news/hookless-vs-hooked-an-explainer), defining bead hooks as "the inward protruding edges at the top of the rim bed that help to retain clincher tyres under pressure"
- Status: `SINGLE SOURCE` for the exact wording, though the underlying concept (hooked vs hookless rims) is widely and consistently described the same way across BikeRadar, LightBicycle and other cycling technical media checked during this research.
- BMX-specific note: no hookless BMX rim was found in this research. Every BMX rim checked is a standard hooked rim. The page states this as a research-limited finding, not as a claim that hookless BMX rims cannot exist.

**Claim: casing TPI (threads per inch) definition and tradeoff**

- Answer: TPI counts threads per inch in the tyre casing. Higher TPI means a lighter, more supple casing; lower TPI means a stiffer casing that is typically more puncture resistant at the same rubber compound.
- Sources:
  - [Kenda Tires, Glossary](https://bicycle.kendatire.com/en-us/tire-tech/glossary/): "A common indicator of the quality of the casing of a tire. Higher TPI tires are lighter weight and provide a more supple ride."
  - [Teravail, "The FYI on TPI"](https://www.teravail.com/blogs/resources/the-fyi-on-tpi-know-your-tire-s-casing), a second tyre manufacturer's technical page, corroborating the weight/suppleness/puncture-resistance tradeoff
- Status: `CONFIRMED` for the general definition and tradeoff.
- Gap: no BMX-specific tyre model's TPI figure was found from any brand checked (Odyssey, Kenda, Fit Bike Co). Odyssey's own Path Pro spec sheet lists only "durable dual ply casing," no TPI number. Published on the site as `UNVERIFIED` for any specific BMX tyre's TPI figure, via a needsverify block.

**Claim: tyre pressure range is per-tyre, illustrated with Odyssey Path Pro**

## Conflicts and cautions

1. **American shell inner diameter is not a single agreed number.** Park Tool and TBB-BIKE say 51.5 mm, Sheldon Brown says 51.3 mm (2.02 in), SkatePro rounds to 51 mm. Part of the spread is that some sources quote the pressed cup and some quote the shell bore. Publish a range, not a single figure.

2. **The Spanish BB bearing part number "6904" is dimensionally wrong as stated.** BMX Direct lists 6904 for both 19 mm and 22 mm Spanish bearings at 37 mm outer diameter, but a stock 6904 is 20 x 37 x 9 mm, a 20 mm bore. TBB-BIKE instead lists "22379 - 2RS", which parses as 22 x 37 x 9 and is coherent. BMX Direct itself flags these as special sizes not stocked by ordinary bearing shops. Recommend listing Spanish bearings by dimensions (37 mm outer diameter, 9 mm width, bore to match spindle) and not by a generic bearing part number.

3. **Euro BB bearing outer diameter is genuinely disputed.** TBB-BIKE says 31 mm, BMX Direct says 32 mm, SkatePro says 35 mm. The 35 mm figure is probably the shell thread size being reported as a bearing size. Because Euro bearings live inside a cup, the bearing size is a function of the cup design, not the frame standard, so a single universal number may not exist. Do not publish one.

4. **Shell widths are poorly documented online.** Euro at 68 mm is well confirmed. Mid and Spanish at 68 mm or 73 mm rests largely on Profile Racing's own product text, which is a good source but is one source. American at 68 mm rests on a single product title from Bicycle Fabrication Supply whose page body could not be read. Treat shell width as less certain than shell diameter.

5. **"19, 22, 24" is commonly presented online as the complete set of BMX spindle sizes. It is not.** Dan's Comp explicitly states that BMX racing also uses 30 mm and 35 mm, and that freestyle in practice uses 19 mm and 22 mm. Any fitment guide that says "BMX spindles come in three sizes" is correct for freestyle and wrong for race.

6. **The 24 mm spindle is often described as simply "the strongest option."** The only source found that addressed the tradeoff, Dan's Comp, says the opposite in part: a 24 mm spindle makes the bearing weaker because the bearing inner diameter grows while the outer diameter is fixed by the shell standard. In BMX today 24 mm is mostly a race, external-bearing, Euro/BSA thing rather than a freestyle Mid thing.

7. **"24 mm" spindle is a rounded imperial size.** Dan's Comp gives it as 15/16 inch, which is 23.81 mm, not 24.00 mm. Worth knowing before measuring a used spindle with calipers and concluding it is undersized.

8. **3/8 inch and 10 mm axles are routinely conflated in listings.** They are 9.525 mm and 10.0 mm respectively. Two sources confirm they are distinct sizes. No source found says they are interchangeable.

9. **Old school versus modern rear dropout spacing could not be verified.** The best forum discussions on this are on BMX Museum and Bike Forums, both of which blocked automated fetching. Sheldon Brown's spacing crib sheet has no BMX row at all. Do not publish a claim in either direction.

10. **Odyssey's own dropout article says 110 mm but then describes 108 mm as "the standard"** in the same piece. Real frames vary. If the site publishes 110 mm, add that measured frames commonly come in slightly under.

11. **Era dating for BB standards is narrative, not dated.** BMX Union describes the sequence (American, then Euro, then Mid, then Spanish) and names specific frames (Kink Cielencki for Euro, FBM PW Moto for Mid) but gives no years. GSport's article is dated June 2004 and describes Spanish as still emerging, which is the single hardest date anchor found. Any specific year published beyond that would be invention.

12. **Sources deliberately not used.** Several high-ranking results for these queries were content-farm style listicles or AI-generated shop blog posts with no attribution. They were excluded. Notably, one widely-cited retailer blog described the standards only by comparison to a coffee cup, a Red Bull can, a penny, a nickel and a quarter, with no measurements at all. That kind of page is likely the origin of a lot of repeated but unsourced numbers online.

13. **Pages that could not be fetched**, and so contributed nothing: BMX Museum forum threads (robots.txt disallowed), Bike Forums threads (403), Bicycle Fabrication Supply product and category pages (robots.txt), Pardo bottom bracket standards page (redirect loop), Source BMX cranks buyers guide (page body did not render), The Pro's Closet BB identification guide (covers road and MTB only, no BMX content).

## Standards citations: two that did not hold up

Two ISO numbers reached published pages as sourcing without anyone opening the
document. Both were wrong, and both were caught only by a later audit.

**ISO 5681** was cited as the source for a spoke gauge figure. ISO 5681 is
"Equipment for crop protection - Vocabulary". Nothing to do with bicycles.
Removed.

**ISO 4210-2** was cited sixteen times across five pillars, as the second
source that made a figure Confirmed: handlebar outer diameter, steerer
diameters, threaded headset thread, front hub over-locknut dimension,
cantilever post spacing, brake lever clamp diameter, saddle rail dimensions
and seat post diameters.

ISO 4210-2 is "Cycles - Safety requirements for bicycles - Part 2". Its own
introduction says the scope "has been limited to safety considerations and has
specifically avoided standardization of components". Its saddle clauses are a
static strength test and a fatigue test, not a rail diameter. It cannot source
any interchange dimension, and it never could.

Checked against the publicly available document at
`https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/78077/7e7a9e3cceeb4c0c9bfa18d139de942b/ISO-4210-2-2023.pdf`
on 29 August 2026.

All sixteen citations were removed. Eight rows kept Confirmed because two real
sources remained. Five dropped to Single source. One, the 1 inch x 24 TPI
threaded headset thread, stayed Confirmed because Park Tool's thread concepts
page independently carries it.

**The rule this produces:** a standards number is not a source until someone
has opened the standard and found the figure in it. A plausible-looking ISO
