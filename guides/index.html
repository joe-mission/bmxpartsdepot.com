The A-Z Parts Glossary

Every term links into the guide section that covers it. All 152 entries are live.

A

  • American Bottom BracketA bottom bracket standard using a large unthreaded frame shell with bearing cups pressed into each side.
  • Axle DiameterThe outside diameter of a hub axle, commonly 14mm on modern rear hubs and 3/8 inch on older and lighter builds.
  • Axle Nuts and BoltsThe threaded hardware that clamps a wheel into the dropouts, either nuts on a male axle or bolts into a female axle.
  • AheadsetA trade name for the threadless headset design that is now the common freestyle standard.
  • Adjustable ConeA threaded cone on a loose-ball hub or headset that sets bearing preload.

B

  • Bottom Bracket Shell WidthThe measured width of the frame's bottom bracket shell between its two faces.
  • Butted TubingTubing with thinner walls in the middle and thicker walls at the ends to save weight without losing strength at the joints.
  • Brake MountsThe welded posts on the seat stays or chain stays that a U-brake bolts onto.
  • Handlebar BacksweepThe angle the bar ends are swept back toward the rider.
  • Sealed Bearing SizesThe numeric code stamped on a cartridge bearing that gives its bore, outside diameter, and width.
  • Bead HookThe lip inside the rim wall that retains the tire bead under pressure, absent on some rim designs and part of what caps safe pressure.
  • 990 MountsThe post spacing standard most BMX U-brakes and frames are built to, and the number needed before a used brake bolts to a used frame.

C

  • Cassette HubA rear hub with an internal ratchet and a removable driver that carries the sprocket-side cog.
  • Chainstay LengthThe distance from the bottom bracket centre to the rear axle.
  • Chain SizeSingle speed BMX chain is normally 1/8 inch wide, with narrower 3/32 inch chain used on some setups.
  • Crank Spindle DiameterThe diameter of the axle running through the bottom bracket, with 19mm, 22mm, and 24mm all in circulation.
  • Chromoly (4130)A chrome-molybdenum steel alloy used for stronger and lighter frames, forks, and bars.
  • Casing TPIThreads per inch in the tire casing, which trades suppleness against puncture resistance and sidewall support.
  • Compression CapThe cap whose bolt pulls the fork up to preload the headset bearings, and which is not a structural clamp.
  • Crown RaceThe bearing race seating on the fork crown, which has to match the headset's bearing angle and diameter or the front end never adjusts properly.
  • CrossbarThe tube bracing a four-piece bar between the uprights, adding stiffness and giving a mount point for a number plate or cable.
  • Cross-ThreadingStarting a fastener off-axis so it cuts across the existing thread, the failure that ruins crank arms and bottom bracket shells.
  • Cable Pull RatioHow much cable a lever pulls per unit of hand movement, which the brake has to be designed for or the lever either hits the bars or the pads never bite.

D

  • Dropout Slot WidthThe width of the axle slot in the frame or fork ends.
  • DriverThe removable splined body on a cassette hub that the cog is part of or bolts to.
  • DetanglerA rotor unit that lets the bars spin while keeping the rear brake connected.
  • Wheel DishHow centred the rim sits between the hub locknuts.
  • DurometerThe rubber hardness rating, which trades grip against wear life and is why two identical-looking tires behave differently.
  • Directional MarkingThe rotation arrow on a directional tire, which decides which way round it mounts and is routinely ignored on used builds.

E

  • Euro Bottom BracketA threaded bottom bracket that screws into a threaded frame shell, common on older race frames.
  • Expander BoltThe long bolt that pulls a wedge up inside a quill stem to lock it in the steerer tube.
  • Effective Top TubeThe horizontal top tube measurement used to compare frame size between brands.
  • Effective Rim DiameterThe diameter a rim measures at the spoke nipple seats, used to calculate spoke length.
  • Hub End CapsThe spacers or caps at each end of a hub axle that set the overall width.

F

  • FreecoasterA rear hub with an internal clutch that lets the bike roll backwards without turning the cranks.
  • FreewheelA threaded cog cluster that screws onto an older rear hub rather than driving from inside it.
  • Female AxleA hub axle that is internally threaded and takes bolts rather than external nuts.
  • Fork OffsetHow far the dropouts sit ahead of the steerer tube centreline, also called rake.
  • Front Load StemA stem with a removable faceplate that clamps the bars from the front, as opposed to a top load.
  • Folding Bead TireA tire with a flexible aramid bead that folds for shipping and saves rotating weight, the lighter and more expensive option.
  • Four-Piece BarBars made from four tubes with a crossbar, the common freestyle construction, with more welds and a different feel under load.

G

  • Gyro TabsThe small welded tabs on the head tube that a detangler bolts to.
  • Gear RatioThe relationship between front sprocket teeth and rear driver or cog teeth.
  • Grip Diameter and FlangeThe inner diameter and end style of a grip, including flanged and flangeless types.
  • Sprocket GuardA plastic or alloy disc that protects the sprocket teeth during grinds.
  • Frame GussetA reinforcing plate welded at a high-stress joint, usually at the head tube or bottom bracket.

H

  • Headset StandardsThe set of head tube and cup designs, including integrated, semi-integrated, and older threaded types.
  • Head Tube AngleThe angle of the head tube relative to the ground.
  • Hi-Ten SteelHigh tensile steel, heavier and cheaper than chromoly, common on entry-level bikes.
  • Hub GuardA disc that sits against the hub to protect the flange and spokes during grinds.
  • Headset Bearing AngleThe contact angles on a cartridge headset bearing, which decide whether a bearing physically seats in a given cup and race combination.
  • Headset Stack HeightHow much height the assembled headset adds above and below the head tube, changing bar height and usable steerer.
  • Headset SpacerSpacers between top race and stem that set bar height and take up spare steerer, which still have to leave the stem fully clamped on the tube.

I

  • Integrated HeadsetA headset where the bearings drop directly into a machined seat inside the head tube with no pressed cups.
  • ISO Bead Seat DiameterThe ISO measurement of the rim diameter at the tire bead seat, the true way to match tire to rim.
  • Integrated Seat ClampA seat clamp built into the frame's seat tube rather than a separate collar.
  • Internal Chain TensionerA tensioner that sits inside the dropout slot to hold axle position under load.
  • ISO 406The bead seat diameter of a standard 20 inch freestyle and street rim, and the number that actually defines 20 inch.
  • ISO 451The larger 20 inch bead seat diameter used on some race and fractional-sized wheels, which will not interchange with 406 in either direction.
  • Inner Tube Size RangeThe span of tire widths a tube is rated for, and why a tube outside its range either folds or over-stretches.
  • Internal Compression BoltA compression system running inside the steerer rather than pulling on a star nut, which is what a hollow-steerer fork needs.

J

  • J-Bend SpokeA spoke with a bent elbow head that hooks through a hub flange, the standard BMX spoke type.
  • Jam NutA second nut tightened against a cone or locknut to stop it backing off.
  • Junior (Race Frame Size)A BMX race frame size class sitting between mini and expert.

K

  • Knurled Bar Clamp AreaThe roughened band in the middle of a handlebar that helps the stem grip.
  • Keyed WasherA washer with a tab that locates in the dropout slot to stop the axle rotating.

L

  • Spoke Lacing PatternThe pattern spokes follow between hub and rim, commonly three cross on BMX.
  • Left Hand DriveA setup where the sprocket, chain, and driver sit on the left side of the bike.
  • Brake Lever ReachHow far the lever blade sits from the bar, sometimes adjustable by a screw.
  • Loose Ball BearingsIndividual bearing balls running in cups and cones rather than in a sealed cartridge.
  • Lock-On GripA grip clamped by a collar rather than held by friction, needing bar end room for the clamp and no glue.
  • Left-Hand ThreadA thread tightening anticlockwise, used where normal rotation would loosen a part, and why a forced pedal or cup comes out damaged.

M

  • Mid Bottom BracketA pressed-in bottom bracket standard using a shell smaller than American, with bearings pressed straight into the frame.
  • Mid-SchoolThe loosely defined era of BMX between old school and modern, marked by specific part standards.
  • Male AxleA hub axle threaded on the outside that takes nuts.
  • Micro DriveA drivetrain using a small front sprocket with a small-tooth-count driver.

N

  • Spoke NippleThe small threaded fitting that joins a spoke to the rim and sets tension.
  • Non-Drive SideThe side of the bike opposite the sprocket and chain.
  • Nylon Pedal BodyA plastic-bodied pedal with a metal spindle, lighter and cheaper than an alloy body.
  • NeckOld-school slang for the stem, still used in listings for period parts.

O

  • One-Piece CrankA crank where both arms and the spindle are a single bent steel piece, also called Ashtabula.
  • Over Locknut DimensionThe measured width of a hub across the outer faces of its locknuts.
  • Old SchoolThe earlier era of BMX, generally covering the sport's first generation of bikes and parts.
  • Oversize Bar ClampA larger-than-standard bar clamp diameter used on some stems and bars.
  • One-Piece to Three-Piece ConversionFitting three-piece cranks into a frame built for one-piece, using an adapter kit in the American shell, the most common upgrade question on old frames.

P

  • Pivotal SeatA seat and post interface using a splined face and a single bolt through the seat.
  • Pedal Thread SizeThe thread size of the pedal spindle, which differs between one-piece and three-piece crank setups.
  • PegsTubes that bolt onto the axle for grinding and standing.
  • Press-Fit Bottom BracketA bottom bracket where bearings or cups press into an unthreaded shell.
  • Pinch BoltA bolt that clamps a slotted part, such as a stem on a steerer or a seat clamp on a post.

Q

  • Quill StemA stem with a long shaft that slides inside a threaded fork steerer and locks with a wedge.
  • Quad-Butted TubingTubing with four different wall thicknesses along its length.
  • Quick Release Brake CableA cable or straddle fitting that unhooks quickly so the wheel can pass the brake pads.

R

  • Rim Wall ConstructionWhether a rim is single, double, or triple wall in cross section.
  • Removable Brake MountsBrake mounts that bolt on rather than being welded to the frame.
  • Right Hand DriveThe conventional setup with the sprocket, chain, and driver on the right side.
  • Handlebar RiseThe vertical height from the bar clamp area to the top of the grip area.
  • Frame ReachThe horizontal distance from the bottom bracket to the head tube top.
  • Rim TapeThe strip covering the spoke nipple holes inside the rim, without which the tube punctures on the nipples rather than from outside.
  • Railed SeatA seat on twin rails clamped by a railed post, the main alternative to pivotal and not cross-compatible with it.

S

  • Spanish Bottom BracketA pressed-in bottom bracket standard with a shell narrower than Mid.
  • Spline Drive SprocketA sprocket that mounts directly to splines on the crank spindle rather than bolting to a spider.
  • Seat Post DiameterThe outside diameter of the seat post, with more than one size in common use.
  • Standover HeightThe height of the top tube above the ground at its lowest useful point.
  • Sprocket Bolt PatternThe number and spacing of mounting bolts on a bolt-on sprocket.
  • Sidewall MarkingThe full set of stamped markings on a tire sidewall and how to read them, which is how a used tire is identified with no packaging.
  • Skinwall TireA tire with an unreinforced tan sidewall, both a period identifier and a real durability difference against a black wall.
  • Steerer Tube DiameterThe outside diameter of the fork's steerer, which has to match both the headset and the stem clamp.
  • Steerer Tube LengthHow much steerer the fork has, which caps how many spacers fit under the stem and whether the fork suits a longer head tube at all.
  • Star NutA sprung nut driven into the steerer giving the top cap something to pull against when setting headset preload on a threadless front end.
  • Split Crown Race RingA split race that clips onto the fork crown rather than being pressed, common on modern BMX and the piece most often missing from a used fork.
  • Stem ReachThe horizontal distance from steerer centre to bar clamp centre, moving the bars forward or back independently of bar sweep.
  • Straddle Cable HangerThe piece carrying the straddle cable above the brake, whose height changes the mechanical advantage of the whole brake.
  • Seat Clamp DiameterThe outside diameter of the seat tube at its top, which is what a clamp must match, and a different number from post diameter.

T

  • Top Tube LengthThe measured length of the top tube, the headline size number on a BMX frame.
  • Threadless HeadsetA headset used with an unthreaded steerer, where a stem clamps the steerer directly.
  • Threaded HeadsetAn older headset design where the top race threads onto a threaded fork steerer.
  • Three-Piece CrankA crankset made of two arms and a separate spindle.
  • Tire Size MarkingHow a tire states its size on the sidewall, where decimal and fractional markings of the same nominal diameter are different standards.
  • Tire SeatingBringing the bead evenly onto its seat so the moulded line sits parallel to the rim, which is what stops a tire running out of round.
  • Tire Pressure RangeThe minimum and maximum pressures marked on the sidewall, which are a property of the tire and rim together rather than rider preference.
  • Tread PatternSlick, semi-slick or knobby, which is a surface decision rather than a fitment one but changes effective width on the same casing.
  • Tube Valve TypeSchrader against Presta, which has to match the rim's valve hole and the pump, and is a different question from the hole's size.
  • Tube GaugeThe wall thickness of a tube, trading weight against puncture resistance, and the difference between a standard and a thorn-resistant tube.
  • Tubeless BMXWhether a given BMX rim and tire can run without a tube, which requires both a compatible bead seat and a sealed rim bed.
  • Top Load StemA threadless stem clamping the bars from above, giving a different bar height and clamping feel than a front load stem on the same fork.
  • Two-Piece BarBars made from two tubes, lighter with fewer welds, distinguishable by eye and both a durability and an era marker.
  • Thread PitchThe distance between thread crests, which makes two same-diameter threads incompatible and is the root of most stripped parts.
  • Torque SpecificationThe tightness a fastener is designed for, which matters more on alloy and titanium parts than on the steel they replace.
  • Thread InsertA helical or solid insert restoring a stripped thread in a frame or crank, the repair deciding whether a damaged part is scrap.
  • Tripod Post SplineA third seat mounting interface using a three-point pattern, compatible with neither railed nor pivotal parts.

U

  • U-BrakeThe standard BMX rim brake, mounted on two posts either side of the wheel.
  • Unsealed BearingsBearings running without a cartridge seal, usually loose ball with cups and cones.
  • Unicrown ForkA fork where the legs bend in to meet the steerer directly, with no separate crown.
  • Handlebar UpsweepThe angle the bar ends rise from the horizontal cross section.

V

  • V-BrakeA linear pull rim brake mounted on posts, uncommon on BMX but found on some builds.
  • Valve Hole SizeThe drilled hole diameter in a rim, sized for either a Schrader or a Presta valve.
  • Vertical DropoutA dropout with a vertical slot rather than a rearward-facing horizontal slot.
  • Valve Stem LengthHow far the valve protrudes past the rim wall, which has to clear deep sections and be long enough to take a pump head.

W

  • WheelbaseThe distance between the front and rear axle centres.
  • Wheel SizeThe nominal wheel diameter of a BMX bike, with 20 inch standard and other sizes used for cruisers and smaller riders.
  • Handlebar WidthThe overall width of the bars measured end to end.
  • Wedge StemA quill stem locked in place by an angled wedge pulled up by the expander bolt.
  • Wire Bead TireA tire with a steel wire bead, heavier, cheaper, and not foldable, which is what most used tires will be.

X

  • 3X LacingThe three cross spoke pattern, where each spoke crosses three others on its way to the rim.
  • Pro XL Frame SizeThe largest common BMX race frame size class.

Y

  • Chainstay YokeThe shaped bridge or plate joining the chainstays near the bottom bracket.
  • Straddle Cable YokeThe small carrier that links the main brake cable to the straddle cable on a U-brake.
  • Yield StrengthThe stress at which a material starts to deform permanently.

Z

  • Zero Stack HeadsetA semi-integrated headset where cups press into the head tube and the bearings sit inside it.
  • Zero Offset Seat PostA seat post with the clamp directly above the post centreline rather than set back.
  • Zinc-Plated HardwareBolts and small parts with a zinc coating for corrosion resistance.
