# BMX Dictionary Expansion: Batch 1 (100 Terms)

This document contains exactly 100 new, high-value technical BMX terms mapped to the 8 strict taxonomy categories. Each term is accompanied by its shop-level mechanical and fitment relevance explanation.

---

### Drivetrain & Gearing (16 Terms)

1. **Sprocket Hat Washer**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** An alloy shim that adapts a sprocket's large center bore (usually 23.8mm/24mm or 22mm) to a smaller crank spindle (19mm or 22mm). Without the correct hat washer, the sprocket cannot sit concentric to the spindle, causing tight and loose spots in the chain tension.

2. **Half Link**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** An offset chain link that measures half the length of a standard link. It is used to adjust the rear wheel position in horizontal dropouts, allowing riders to slam their wheel as far forward as possible to shorten the wheelbase.

3. **Chain Elongation**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** The physical lengthening of a chain caused by pin and bushing wear (often called "stretch"). Replacing a worn chain late will rapidly wear down the teeth of new sprockets and expensive cassette drivers.

4. **Points of Engagement (POE)**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** The number of times the pawls inside a cassette hub driver can engage with the drive ring per one full revolution of the wheel. Higher POE reduces crank dead-spot play, giving immediate power transfer during pedal pressure.

5. **Freecoaster Clutch Slack**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** The amount of forward rotation required before a freecoaster hub's internal clutch engages to drive the wheel. This slack is adjustable using spacer washers behind the clutch to prevent accidental engagement during fakie tricks.

6. **Crank Spindle Spline Count**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** The number of teeth machined onto a crank axle (typically 8, 16, or 48 splines) that match the splines inside the crank arms. Sprockets must match this spline count if they are spline-driven rather than bolt-driven.

7. **Spline Drive**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** A mounting interface where a sprocket's center bore matches the splines of the crank spindle directly, eliminating the need for a drive bolt. This setup distributes drive forces evenly across all splines, preventing loose sprocket bolts.

8. **Bolt-on Sprocket (Drive Bolt)**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** A sprocket that attaches to a crank arm using a single drive bolt threaded into the arm. Mismatching the drive bolt interface or overtightening can strip the crank arm or leave the chainline offset.

9. **1/2" x 1/8" Chain Pitch**
   * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
   * **Relevance:** The standard heavy-duty chain size for freestyle BMX, representing a 1/2-inch spacing between pins and a 1/8-inch internal width. Freestyle sprockets and drivers must use this width, whereas race setups occasionally use narrower 3/32-inch chains.

10. **Ashtabula Spindle Thread**
    * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
    * **Relevance:** The unique 24 TPI threaded portion on old-school 1-piece cranks that carries the bottom bracket cones. Mismatching this threaded standard with ISO or metric parts will strip the threads instantly.

11. **Chainline Offset**
    * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
    * **Relevance:** The lateral distance between the centerline of the frame and the chain path. A crooked chainline causes noisy operation, accelerated sprocket wear, and increases the likelihood of throwing the chain under load.

12. **Driver Pawl Spring**
    * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
    * **Relevance:** The tiny wire spring or leaf spring that pushes the cassette pawls outward into the hub's drive ring. A broken or rusted spring leads to driver slipping, which can cause sudden crank slips and rider injury.

13. **Spindle Spacer Shims**
    * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
    * **Relevance:** Thin, 19mm, 22mm, or 24mm ID washers placed on the crank spindle between the bottom bracket bearings and the crank arms. They are used to align the sprocket perfectly with the rear driver and adjust arm clearance from the chainstays.

14. **Q-Factor (Crank Width)**
    * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
    * **Relevance:** The lateral distance between the outside of the crank arms at the pedal attachment point. A mismatch in spindle spacers or crank arm design can lead to unequal Q-factor, causing heel-clash against the frame chainstays.

15. **9T Driver Standard**
    * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
    * **Relevance:** The modern standard gear size for rear cassette and freecoaster hubs, which allows for smaller front sprockets (like 25T) to save weight and maximize grinding clearance. A 9T driver is integrated into the hub and cannot be swapped for larger tooth counts without changing the driver body.

16. **Freewheel Thread Standard (1.375" x 24 TPI)**
    * **Category:** Drivetrain & Gearing
    * **Relevance:** The threaded standard used on old-school hubs to screw on freewheels with 16 or more teeth. Mismatching this with metric BMX race hubs (which use smaller M30 threads for 13T-15T cogs) makes it impossible to secure the sprocket.

### Frame Geometry & Construction (13 Terms)

17. **Seatstay Bridge**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** The structural tube joining the seatstays above the rear tire. The height of this bridge dictates maximum tire height and provides the reinforcement necessary to resist frame twist under heavy side loads.

18. **Investment Cast Dropouts**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** Dropouts formed using molten metal in a mold rather than being welded plates. They allow for clean, seamless tube junctions, integrated chain tensioners, and thicker construction that resists bending during grind impacts.

19. **Wishbone Chainstay**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** A frame design where the two chainstays merge into a single solid yoke or tube before connecting to the bottom bracket shell. This increases lateral rear-end stiffness and provides extra clearance for wider modern tires.

20. **Double-Butted Tubing**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** Steel tubes that are thicker at the ends (near welds) and thinner in the middle. This reduces overall frame weight without compromising the strength of high-stress weld areas.

21. **Post-Weld Heat Treatment**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** Heating the entire chromoly frame in an oven after welding to relieve molecular stresses introduced by the welding torch. This process significantly increases impact resistance at the tube joints.

22. **Integrated Chain Tensioners**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** Screws built directly inside the dropout slots that push the axle backward. They prevent the wheel from slipping forward under hard pedaling and allow for precise chain tension adjustment without external tensioner plates.

23. **Seatpost Minimum Insertion**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** The safety line marked on a seatpost indicating the minimum length that must remain inside the seat tube. Running a post too high can crack the frame's seat tube junction during hard impacts.

24. **Standover Height**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** The vertical distance from the center of the bottom bracket shell to the center of the top tube at the seat tube junction. This defines how tall the frame frame looks and affects knee clearance during tricks.

25. **Bottom Bracket Height**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** The distance from the center of the bottom bracket spindle to the flat ground. A higher BB height raises the center of gravity for quicker spinning, while a lower BB height increases stability.

26. **Chainstay Length (Slammed)**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** The distance from the BB center to the rear axle when the wheel is pulled as far forward in the dropout as possible. Shorter slammed stays make the bike hop and manual much easier.

27. **Headtube Angle**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** The angle of the steering column relative to the ground. Steeper head angles (e.g., 75.5°) make steering quicker and facilitate nose-manuals, whereas slacker angles (e.g., 74°) improve high-speed stability.

28. **Headtube Stack (Length)**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** The overall height of the frame's headtube. Newer frames use taller headtubes (e.g., 117mm or 120mm) to eliminate the need for stack spacers under the stem, changing the minimum fork steerer length required.

29. **Removable Gyro Tabs Holes**
    * **Category:** Frame Geometry & Construction
    * **Relevance:** Small threaded holes drilled into the sides of the headtube to accept bolt-on detangler tabs. If a frame lacks these holes, a clamp-on headset gyro plate must be used instead.

### Wheels, Hubs & Axles (15 Terms)

30. **Bead Seat Diameter (BSD)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The true diameter of the rim where the tire bead seats (e.g., 406mm for standard 20" tires and 451mm for race 20"). Mismatching the tire and rim BSD makes mounting impossible or causes the tire to blow off.

31. **Hub Flange Diameter (PCD)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The diameter of the circle formed by the spoke holes in the hub flange. This is a critical measurement for calculating spoke length; high-flange and low-flange hubs require completely different spoke sets.

32. **Flange Spacing**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The lateral distance between the left and right flanges of a hub. Wider spacing yields a stiffer wheel but requires careful calculation of spoke length for both the drive and non-drive sides.

33. **Spoke Gauge (14G vs. 15G)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The thickness of the spoke wire, with 14G (2.0mm) being the standard heavy-duty freestyle size. Using thinner 15G (1.8mm) spokes requires matching 15G spoke nipples, or the threads will strip.

34. **Directional Rim Drilling**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** Rim spoke holes drilled at alternating angles matching the path of spokes coming from the hub flanges. Lacing a directional rim backward bends the spokes at the nipple, causing frequent spoke breakage.

35. **Brass vs. Alloy Nipples**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** Brass nipples are heavier but resist stripping and corrosion, making them ideal for street riding. Alloy nipples save weight at the wheel's outer edge but strip easily under high tension.

36. **Hub Guard Drive-Side (DS)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** A protective guard shaped to clear the chain and sprocket while shielding the drive-side hub flange during grinds. Mismatching DS guards will bind the chain against the driver.

37. **Female Hub Bolt Thread (3/8" x 24 TPI)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The standard thread pitch for front female axle bolts. Using standard hardware store metric M10 bolts will cross-thread and ruin the internal threads of the chromoly axle spindle.

38. **Wheel Dish**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The lateral centering of the rim relative to the outer edges of the hub locknuts. An improperly dished wheel will sit off-center in the frame, causing tire rub and brake misalignment.

39. **Single-Wall vs. Double-Wall Rim**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** Single-wall rims have one layer of metal, making them light but weak. Double-wall rims have an internal chamber that resists buckle and dent damage under hard street landings.

40. **Rim Strip Width**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The width of the protective band covering the spoke nipples inside the rim. If the strip is too narrow, it slips aside, exposing the tube to sharp nipple edges and causing flats.

41. **Axle Locknut (OLD Spacer)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The outer nuts on the hub axle that seat against the inside of the frame dropouts. Swapping locknut spacers changes the hub's spacing to fit different frame widths.

42. **Kevlar Bead Tire**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** Tires that use flexible Kevlar threads instead of steel wire for the bead. They are significantly lighter and foldable, but require careful seating to prevent tire blowouts.

43. **Rim Joint (Welded vs. Pinned)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** Pinned rims use a small metal pin to connect the rim loop, while welded rims are fused. Welded rims are much stronger at the seam and prevent flat spots during hard impacts.

44. **Axle Thread Pitch (14mm x 1.0mm)**
    * **Category:** Wheels, Hubs & Axles
    * **Relevance:** The standard fine thread used on 14mm male BMX rear axles. Mismatching this thread with standard coarse metric hardware will ruin the axle threads beyond repair.

### Steering & Front End (12 Terms)

45. **Integrated Headset Campy Spec (45°/45°)**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** The standard integrated headset bearing size (41.8mm OD) with 45-degree inner and outer angles. Drop-in frame seats are cut precisely to this angle, meaning 36°/45° bearings will wobble and ruin the frame.

46. **Crown Race Seat (30.0mm)**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** The machined shelf at the base of the fork steerer where the crown race is pressed. A worn or undersized seat lets the race spin, causing steering play and headset wear.

47. **Fork Offset (Rake)**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** The distance from the center of the fork dropouts to the centerline of the steerer tube. Short offsets (15mm-22mm) make front-end tricks easier, while long offsets (32mm) increase stability.

48. **Integrated Crown Race**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** Forks with a built-in 45-degree angle machined directly at the base of the steerer tube. This design eliminates the need for a separate pressed crown race and simplifies headset assembly.

49. **Fork Steerer Thread (M24 x 1.5)**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** The thread specification inside the top of the fork steerer tube for the compression cap. Swapping M24 cap threads with M25 or M18 caps will strip the fork threads.

50. **Stem Stack Height**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** The vertical height of the stem's steerer clamp. The fork steerer tube must be long enough to cover at least 90% of the stem's stack height to ensure a safe clamping force.

51. **Stem Rise (Top Load vs. Front Load)**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** How high the handlebar clamp center sits relative to the steerer clamp. Top load stems lift the bars higher, while front load stems drop the bars for a lower cockpit setup.

52. **Star Nut Clamping**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** A star-shaped steel anchor driven into the steerer tube of older or entry-level forks. It provides the threads for the top cap to preload the headset bearings before the stem is clamped.

53. **Knurled Stem Clamp Area**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** The cross-hatched texture in the center of the handlebars. Worn or flattened knurling reduces clamping friction, making the bars slip forward or backward under heavy impacts.

54. **Fork Tapered Legs**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** Fork legs that shrink in diameter toward the dropouts. This clears room for wide tires and saves weight while maintaining strength at the high-stress crown junction.

55. **Headset Spacer Stack**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** Spacers placed under or over the stem to adjust handlebar height. The total stack height must leave the top stem bolt clamping fully onto the solid fork steerer tube.

56. **Aheadset Design**
    * **Category:** Steering & Front End
    * **Relevance:** The threadless headset system where the stem clamps onto the outside of the fork steerer tube, clamping the headset bearings. This system is lighter and stronger than older threaded setups.

### Braking Systems & Detanglers (12 Terms)

57. **990 Mount Spacing**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** The distance between the brake posts (usually 76mm-80mm). If the posts are welded too close or too far apart, the brake pads will not align with the rim braking surface.

58. **Removable Brake Mounts**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** Threaded bosses that screw into the frame stays rather than being permanently welded. If a rider loses the specific brand-compatible mounts, they cannot run rear brakes.

59. **Gyro Detangler Bearing**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** The center rotor ring that spins on ball bearings, allowing the handlebars to rotate infinitely without wrapping the rear brake cable. A dry or gritty bearing causes stiff braking.

60. **Dual Upper Cable**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** A cable setup running from the brake lever directly to both sides of the detangler rotor. It provides a stiffer, more responsive brake feel than 2-into-1 split cables.

61. **London Mod**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** A routing plate that splits the lower brake cable into two separate inner wires, connecting directly to each brake arm. This bypasses the flexible straddle cable, resulting in maximum stopping power.

62. **Straddle Cable Yoke**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** The plate connecting the main pull cable to the transverse straddle cable on U-brakes. A missing or improperly aligned yoke causes uneven pad pull and poor braking.

63. **Clear Brake Pads**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** Soft compound brake pads designed specifically for colored or painted rims. They offer superior grab on painted surfaces but wear down faster and squeal loudly when wet.

64. **Brake Lever Clamp (22.2mm)**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** The standard handlebar clamp diameter for brake levers. Attempting to fit a 22.2mm lever onto a non-BMX bar with a different diameter will fail to clamp securely.

65. **Brake Arm Brass Bushings**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** Small brass sleeves inside the brake arms that rotate on the brake posts. Worn bushings allow the brake arms to wobble, causing uneven pad wear and brake chatter.

66. **Dual Lower Cable**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** The lower routing system for a gyro setup that connects both sides of the rotor to the rear brake. Mismatching cable lengths will cause the brake to rub when turning the bars.

67. **Caliper Reach**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** The distance from the mounting bolt of a caliper brake to the center of the brake pads. A brake with too short of a reach will compress the pads directly into the tire sidewall, causing a blowout.

68. **Brake Mount Adaptor Plate**
    * **Category:** Braking Systems & Detanglers
    * **Relevance:** A bolt-on plate that clamps to a brakeless frame's chainstays or seatstays to add U-brake posts. These plates add weight and flex, reducing overall braking power.

### Seating & Cockpit (12 Terms)

69. **Pivotal Seat Standard**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** A seat-to-post interface featuring matching splined arcs locked together by a single top bolt. A Pivotal seat will not mount to a railed, Tripod, or Stealth post.

70. **Stealth Pivotal**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** A variation of the Pivotal system where the mounting bolt threads in from the bottom of the seatpost rather than through the top of the seat, creating a clean look.

71. **Tripod Seat Standard**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** A seat-to-post system using a three-point wedge plate that offers two preset seat angles. It is lightweight but completely incompatible with Pivotal or railed setups.

72. **Seatpost Shim**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** A thin metal sleeve used to adapt a small diameter seatpost (like 25.4mm) to fit a larger frame seat tube (like 27.2mm). A loose or missing shim allows the seat to slip.

73. **Handlebar Rise**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** The vertical distance from the center of the stem clamp area to the center of the grip ends. This determines the rider's posture and front-end lift leverage.

74. **Handlebar Backsweep**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** The angle at which the handlebar grips sweep back toward the rider. Proper backsweep alignment reduces wrist fatigue and changes the rider's cockpit reach.

75. **Seatpost Clamp Size (28.6mm)**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** The inside diameter of the seat clamp, matching the frame's external seat tube diameter. Using a clamp that is too large will fail to hold the seatpost in place.

76. **Integrated Seat Clamp Bolt**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** A seatpost clamping mechanism built directly into the frame using a small bolt and nut. Stripping this bolt requires a replacement frame-specific nut or thread repair insert.

77. **Flangeless Grips**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** Grips lacking a raised inner rubber flange, common in modern street riding to prevent interference with hands during barspins. Fitment is universal on standard 22.2mm bar ends.

78. **4-Piece Handlebars**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** Handlebars constructed from four separate welded steel tubes instead of two. They provide a stiffer feel and a classic look, but are slightly heavier.

79. **Nylon Bar Ends**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** Plastic plugs pressed into the open ends of the handlebars to protect the rider from serious injury during crashes. Bars must not be ridden without bar ends.

80. **Lock-On Grip Collar**
    * **Category:** Seating & Cockpit
    * **Relevance:** Grips that lock onto the bars via metal clamps on each end. They prevent grip slip but require a clean, unbent bar end for the lockring to slide over.

### Vintage & Era Identification (11 Terms)

81. **Ashtabula 1-Piece Crank**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** A classic old-school crankset made from a single forged steel bar. They require a large American BB shell and use unique 1/2-inch pedal threads.

82. **Looptail Rear End**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** An old-school frame design where the seatstays and chainstays form a continuous loop at the dropouts. This is a key era marker for dating vintage frames.

83. **Threaded Fork Steerer (1")**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** A vintage fork steerer tube with threads cut onto its outside diameter, requiring a matching threaded headset and quill stem. This standard cannot be mixed with modern threadless parts.

84. **Quill Stem Wedge**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** An angled block at the bottom of a quill stem. When the expander bolt is tightened, the wedge slides sideways, locking the stem inside the threaded fork steerer.

85. **Tuff Wheels (Mag Wheels)**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** Spokeless composite wheels made from nylon and fiberglass. They are a signature old-school style choice and require unique axle sets that differ from modern hubs.

86. **Web Gusset**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** A reinforcement plate welded between the frame tubes (often top tube and headtube) to prevent bending. They are a classic design marker used to identify vintage manufacturers.

87. **JIS 27.0mm vs. ISO 26.4mm Crown Race**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** Two competing old-school 1-inch crown race standards. Trying to force an ISO race onto a JIS fork seat will split the race, while a JIS race on an ISO fork will wobble.

88. **Layback Seatpost**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** A seatpost bent backward to extend the rider's cockpit room. This is a classic old-school style part that shifts the rider's center of gravity rearward.

89. **Coaster Brake Arm**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** The metal arm extending from a coaster brake hub that clips to the frame's chainstay. Without this arm secured, pedaling backward will spin the axle instead of braking.

90. **New Old Stock (NOS)**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** Original vintage parts that were manufactured decades ago but never sold or used. They carry high collector value but must be checked for dry rot and dry grease.

91. **Threaded Headset Cup Press (1")**
    * **Category:** Vintage & Era Identification
    * **Relevance:** The pressed cup diameter of vintage 1-inch headsets (usually 30.2mm). Mismatching these cups with frames built for JIS 29.8mm cups will damage the headtube.

### Hardware, Fasteners & Tools (9 Terms)

92. **Spoke Wrench Size (3.2mm vs. 3.45mm)**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** The size of the slot in the spoke wrench. Using a 3.45mm wrench on a 3.2mm nipple will round off the brass nipple flats, preventing the wheel from being trued.

93. **Bottom Bracket Press Tool**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** A threaded tool with drift plates used to press Mid or Spanish bearings straight into the frame shell. Hammering bearings in directly will damage the inner races.

94. **Chain Breaker Tool (1/8")**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** A screw-driven tool used to push out chain pins. Using a tool designed for thin multi-speed chains on a thick 1/8" BMX chain will bend the tool pin or damage the links.

95. **Peg Socket Extension**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** A long extension bar needed to reach the axle nut located deep inside the peg body. Without it, the axle nut cannot be reached or tightened securely.

96. **Crank Spindle Bolt (M12 x 1.25)**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** The thread standard for crank spindle arm-fixing bolts. Mismatching this thread pitch with standard hardware store bolts will strip the internal spindle threads.

97. **Pedal Washer**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** A thin steel washer placed between the pedal spindle shoulder and the crank arm. It prevents the steel spindle from gouging and cracking alloy crank arms.

98. **Cone Wrench flats**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** Thin, flat wrench slots on hub cones. Using a standard thick open-ended wrench will prevent adjusting the locknut, requiring thin cone wrenches instead.

99. **Chain Tensioner Screws (M5 vs. M6)**
    * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
    * **Relevance:** The small bolts inside dropouts used to set chain tension. Replacing them with the wrong thread pitch will strip the frame's integrated dropout threads.

100. **Thread Insert (Helicoil)**
     * **Category:** Hardware, Fasteners & Tools
     * **Relevance:** A coil insert used to repair stripped threads (like in crank arms or brake mounts). A properly installed insert allows the component to be saved instead of discarded.
