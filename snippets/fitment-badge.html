Not sure if this spindle fits your frame?
Your frame's shell diameter sets the bearing outer diameter. Your cranks set the bore. Both have to match.
Measure two things: the inside diameter of your frame's bottom bracket shell, and the diameter of your crank spindle where it passes through. Message me both numbers before buying if you are unsure and I will tell you straight whether this one works.
14mm, 3/8 inch, or 10mm axle?
These are three different sizes, not three names for the same one. 3/8 inch is 9.525mm, which is not a 10mm axle, and pegs bored for one will not slide onto another.
Measure: put a caliper across the axle where the peg or nut sits. Do it before ordering pegs or a replacement axle.
Working out what this frame will take?
Bottom bracket standard, headset type, brake mounts, and dropout slot width all decide what bolts on.
Ask before you buy. Tell me the parts you are planning to move over and I will measure the relevant dimensions on this frame and report the numbers.
Will this sprocket fit your cranks?
Sprocket bore and spindle diameter have to match. Plenty of sprockets are bored for the largest size and use an adapter to drop down, so the bore alone does not tell you what it came off.
Measure: the diameter of your crank spindle. Then check it against the bore listed above.
Threadless or threaded front end?
A quill stem drops inside the steerer tube and locks with a wedge. A threadless stem clamps around the outside of it. They do not swap without changing the fork.
Check: look at your existing stem. If it disappears into the frame, you are threaded. If it wraps around a tube sticking up, you are threadless.
Pivotal, railed, or tripod?
The seat and the post have to use the same mounting system. These three are not cross-compatible with each other in any direction.
Check two things: how your current seat attaches to its post, and the diameter of the post itself. Post diameter also has to match your frame's seat tube.
Check the fitment before you buy
BMX standards changed a lot between the eighties and now, and parts that look interchangeable often are not.
